# 15. User interface

You are here: the page the owner opens with morning coffee. Chapter 14 specifies static Razor rendering enhanced by plain JavaScript and CSS, with no Node/npm frontend tooling. This chapter defines behavior and appearance. The visual tokens below define the shared product design; personal categories, views and background choices come from the instance.

Source map: [Razor components](../src/Feed.Web/Components) · [CSS](../src/Feed.Web/wwwroot/css/feed.css) · [JavaScript modules](../src/Feed.Web/wwwroot/js) · [LayoutTests](../tests/Feed.Tests/LayoutTests.cs).

**Contract versus mechanism.** The visual tokens, layout, chronology, controls, finite-feed behavior, accessibility and interaction outcomes are product requirements. DOM/component structure, CSS implementation and event-observation/debounce techniques may differ when they preserve those outcomes and the no-frontend-toolchain constraint. Reference techniques below are not permission to redesign the page or omit content for performance.

## 15.1 First impression

The page is dark and calm. A full-page photograph fills the viewport behind everything (chapter 14 picks it from one local/Bing pool, or uses the owner's pin). Apply `backgrounds.blur_px` (default 4) to this image layer only. Extend the layer beyond every viewport edge by enough to keep the blur's soft edge off screen, and clip overflow so it creates no scrollbar. Feed text and controls remain sharp. Content floats above it on translucent dark panels with heavy blur, large rounded corners and soft shadows: the glass look. Text is light gray on dark. Blue is the only strong accent. One warm element exists: a small gold sun in the mark.

Content sits in a centered column, at most 1280 px wide, with 20 px side padding (12 px under 480 px of viewport). The feed is a grid of cards in columns. A desktop shows three columns, a narrow window two, a phone one. There is no light theme.

## 15.2 Identity

- **The mark.** A sun coming up over the horizon, its rays drawn as the arcs of a feed: the friends' page with morning coffee, a reader the owner owns. Four shapes in a 24-unit box: a near arc (radius 8) and a far arc (radius 11.5, at 55% opacity), both in the soft accent with round caps; a half-disc sun in gold; a horizon bar (18 by 2, rounded) in the text color. Rendered at 22 px in the bar. Drawn inline, no icon library.
- **The wordmark.** The name "Feed" set in the UI face, weight 700, letter-spacing tightened by a hundredth of an em.
- **The favicon.** The same drawing on the page's dark, in a rounded square, as SVG.
- **Tokens.** Accent `#4c8dff` for every active state and the Collect button. Deep accent `#2f6fe0` for the far end of the Collect gradient and the open menu. Soft accent `#8cbcff` for links, badges, the mark's arcs and the debug page's headings and links. Sun `#f6c453` for the mark and the avatar fallback. Text `#eef1f5`. Page base `#10151d`.
- **Glass.** The top bar `rgba(14,18,26,0.62)` with a 16 px blur. Cards `rgba(16,21,30,0.72)` with a 14 px blur, a 1 px border at 8% white, an 18 px radius, a shadow `0 10px 30px rgba(0,0,0,0.35)`.
- **Type.** The system UI face. No web font. Body text 0.95 rem at line height 1.45.
- **Radii.** 6 for the summary box, 8 for tags, 10 for buttons and tiles, 12 for media and inner panels, 14 for banners and the stack sheet, 18 for cards and the top bar. Pills and chips are fully round.
- **Motion.** One transition: a dropdown caret (Collect, view, platform) rotates 180 degrees in 120 ms when its panel opens. Nothing else animates.
- **Status colors** on the debug page are semantic, not brand: green ok, red error, blue pending, amber running.

## 15.3 The toolbar

A glass bar spans the top of the column and stays pinned while the feed scrolls. It has two lines on a wide page and one line on a narrow one.

**Line one, left: the brand block.** The mark, the wordmark, and a stats line in small muted text: `<n> posts · <n> friends[ · <n> ads dropped][ · last collect <age> ago (+<new>)]`. The post count is the live selection of the current view and platform. Friends and ads are per platform scope.

**Line one, right: inspection links.** Three quiet gray text links, not daily navigation: `Unsorted (n)` (only while the model is enabled; tooltip "Posts waiting for the model's verdict; not shown in any category view"), `Hidden (n)` (tooltip "Debug: inspect hidden posts and why"), `Debug` (tooltip "Live debug: runs, scheduler, processes"). The link of an inspection mode that is on shows as a small translucent blue pill with white text. Both desktop and mobile Debug links show a small amber dot, with an accessible label and tooltip, while there are processing diagnostics, recent upstream warnings, waiting judgments, stale/incomplete coverage or a re-login requirement.

**Line two: the views.** One pill per view in taxonomy order, then `All` last. The active view is a solid accent pill with white text; the others are plain text on a faint group background. A click leaves any inspection mode. The labels come only from the instance. A generic example is `Home`, `People`, `Category A`, `Category B`, `Rare voices`, `All`.

**Line two: the platforms.** A second pill group of glyphs, not words: a four-square glyph for all platforms, then one brand glyph per platform that has stored posts, drawn inline. Each glyph carries the platform name as its tooltip and accessible label. A paused platform is absent from the picker; when the owner opens it by URL its glyph returns dimmed with the tooltip "Paused by config: not in the default feed; this shows its stored posts". A platform without a glyph shows its name.

**Line two, right edge: Mark all read.** A quiet ghost button. It moves the new-posts divider to now. It sits at the right edge together with Collect, pushed there by the free space.

**Line two, right edge: the Collect menu.** A prominent button with a 135 degree gradient from the accent to the deep accent, white text, weight 600, a caret. Open, it is solid deep accent and the caret points up. Its panel (min 260 px wide) holds `Collect now`, then one item per enabled platform and mode: `Collect Instagram · Home`, `Collect Facebook · Close friends`, and so on. The button label always states the truth: `Collect` idle, `Collect requested ✓` while queued, `Collecting…` while a run is in progress. Items are disabled while their platform runs, is requested, or needs re-login, with the reason in the tooltip; an item in cooldown stays enabled and its tooltip says when the run will start. `Collect now` is disabled when no target is open, with the reason. The menu closes on an outside click, on Escape, and before a submit.

Dropdown panels sit 8 px under the button, right-aligned; the view picker's panel in the one-row bar is left-aligned. They are at least 200 px wide and at most 420 px or 58% of the viewport high, with a scroll. They have an 8 px padding, a 12 px radius, near-opaque dark glass and a strong shadow. Rows are 7 by 10 px with a 10 px radius; the active row is solid accent; a dimmed row is at 55% opacity.

**The one-row bar.** When the feed is one column, the bar is one row. The switch is the feed's own threshold: the column narrower than 698 px, the width two columns need, measured on the page column rather than the viewport, so the scrollbar does not shift it. Line one is not shown. The row holds, left to right:

- a view dropdown whose button reads the current view's name, takes the width that is left, and clips a long name with an ellipsis;
- a platform dropdown whose button shows the active glyph (tooltip "Platform: <name>");
- the read button as a check mark, its name in the tooltip;
- the Collect button with the shorter queued label `Requested ✓`.

Each dropdown opens a panel of rows, the active one in solid accent, and closes like the Collect menu. The dropdowns must work without script.

## 15.4 Banners

Amber panels between the toolbar and the feed, when needed:

- **Re-login needed:** `Re-login needed: <platforms>. Run Feed.Cli login --platform <platform> and finish the login in the noVNC browser.`
- **Routine diagnostics:** processing queues/retries, upstream warnings and stale/incomplete coverage appear in Debug, with a small dot on its menu link. They do not create banners on the normal feed. Reserve normal-feed banners for important issues requiring owner action, such as re-login. A re-login banner must not imply that already captured posts cannot be processed.
- **Unsorted mode:** `Unsorted: <n> posts waiting for a judgment of their current content (<n> in this scope).` with a link back to the live feed. Successful judgments place passing posts into matching category views; a judgment can also hide a post under the owner's policy.
- **Hidden mode:** `Debug: showing <n> hidden posts (<n> total). Each card states which filter hid it and why.` with a link back to the live feed.

## 15.5 The feed area

- **Columns.** A masonry grid with an 18 px gap. The column count is the largest that fits with columns of at least 340 px: `floor((width + 18) / 358)`. Three at the full 1240 px content width, two from 698 px, one below. Each unit, in feed order, goes into the currently shortest column. Cards keep their natural height; ragged bottom edges are the point. The newest posts sit at the top of all columns, not only the first.
- **Relayout.** A resize or an unsized image load updates the layout while keeping the topmost visible card at the same viewport offset. Expanding a card grows only its own column; the layout never jumps. An 80 ms debounce and CSS content visibility are reference techniques for reducing work. Resize observers, incremental layout or equivalent browser techniques may replace them if reading order, anchoring and smooth scrolling hold. All selected content remains in the server-rendered page; optimization does not introduce paging, infinite scroll or inaccessible virtualized-away cards.
- **Without script:** server-rendered cards fall back to rows in reading order, newest first. Navigation, text, expansion, media links and form actions remain usable.
- **The divider.** A thin centered line across the grid, in live mode: `new posts above · <age of the oldest new post> and older below`, for example `3 h ago and older below` or `12 Sep 2026 and older below`. It marks what is new since the last "mark all read". The grid above and the grid below are two independent layouts.
- **Finite.** The page renders the newest `ui.render_cap` posts (default 300), then ends. The owner can raise this instance budget, for example to 100000, so a matching view below that count renders in full. Do not clamp it back to 300. No paging, no infinite scroll, no day separators. Images load lazily; a large All page still carries the cost of its full HTML and DOM, particularly on a phone. The default remains a finite 300-post feed.
- **Empty state.** When there is nothing to show, one centered glass panel (dashed border, muted text) names the empty scope and links to a wider one:

| Scope | Message and links |
|---|---|
| hidden mode | `No hidden posts[ on <Platform>].` |
| unsorted mode | `Nothing is waiting for the model[ on <Platform>].` |
| a platform is set | any of the below, plus a link `show all platforms` |
| a category view | `No posts judged as <Label>[ on <Platform>] yet.` plus `<n> posts wait for the model in Unsorted` when n > 0 |
| a rare view | `No rare voices[ on <Platform>] right now.` plus the Unsorted link when the model is on and n > 0 |
| a union view | `Nothing in the <Label> view[ on <Platform>] yet.` |
| an author view | `No posts from the people in the <Label> view[ on <Platform>].` |
| all, a platform set | `No posts on <Platform> in the feed.` |
| all, model on, unjudged exist | `Nothing judged yet.` plus a link to Unsorted |
| otherwise | `The feed is empty. Nothing collected yet.` plus the blue `Collect now` button, the only case with the button |

## 15.6 The card

One glass card per post, 16 px padding (12 at the bottom), top to bottom:

1. **Hidden tag** (hidden mode only). A small amber tag at the top: `hidden · <owner>: <reason>`. The card is dimmed to 55% with a dashed amber outline.
2. **Head.** A round 40 px avatar, or a letter tile (the first letter of the name, weight 700, dark text on a gradient from the soft accent to the sun). The author name (weight 650) links to the profile in a new tab. A small muted meta line: the platform in uppercase small caps, the relative time with the UTC time as tooltip, then badges. Badges are small colored pills: `sponsored` red, `suggested` amber, `reel` blue, `event` violet; a teal "why here" badge (tooltip "Why this post is in this view") in a union view or a rare view (chapter 10); an orange stack badge (tooltip "This author's burst is folded behind this post").
3. **Summary box** (only when a summary exists). A left-bordered box with a tiny uppercase `summary` label and the 1 to 2 sentence summary in italics. Bare URLs in it are links.
4. **The full text**, collapsed when a summary exists, otherwise shown:
   - the with-line, `with A and B and C`, names linked to profiles;
   - the caption, whitespace preserved, links in the soft accent, tagged people as bold links;
   - the shared block: an inner panel labeled `shared` with the original author (linked) and the shared text, so the reader never mistakes it for the author's words;
   - the memory block: the same panel with a violet tint, labeled `memory · <label>`.
5. **Media**, always visible, in post order, in one mosaic. Rounded corners, 4 px gaps, 6 px vertical margin.
   - One item: full card width. A single image opens the lightbox. A single video is a native player with controls. With a poster it downloads nothing until play; without one it loads its metadata for the first frame. A post with exactly one image and a video shows the image as the video's poster, at the image's size, so cover and video are one block that never changes height.
   - Two items: two square tiles side by side.
   - Three or more: one large tile on the left spanning two rows, two small tiles stacked on the right, the whole grid at 3:2. A `+N` scrim (1.5 rem, weight 700, white on a dark translucent overlay) on the third tile when more items exist.
   - A video tile is a still: no controls, muted, its poster or else the first frame of the file (which costs the metadata and nothing more). A play badge (a triangle in a 44 px dark circle) is centered on it and takes no clicks. The badge is hidden under the `+N` scrim.
   - The post's first video is always one of the tiles. When it comes after the third item (an Instagram carousel's video is numbered after all its pictures), it takes the second tile, the third tile shows the second picture, and the `+N` scrim opens the lightbox at the first item the tiles skip.
   - The two-tile and three-tile mosaics have a fixed aspect, so a card with a video among them never changes height after load. A single video without a poster has no size until its metadata arrives; store the video's width and height at download when they can be read, and give the player those attributes.
   - A tile click opens the lightbox at that item. Without script, every item the tiles do not show is reachable as a link.
6. **Retention line** (when a file was pruned): one muted italic line, `one media file not stored` or `<n> media files not stored`, with the reason available (retention, configured size cap, unavailable tool or download failure), plus `, the original is linked below` when a permalink exists.
7. **Expand chip** (only with a summary), between the media and the footer: `show full text`, then `show less`. It carries the expanded state for assistive technology. Expanding grows this card only. The collapsed state is painted directly; the page never paints the full text and then collapses it.
8. **Footer.** A thin rule, then one row of compact round chips (0.8 rem, translucent white, brighter on hover). The row does not wrap at a column's width. Left to right:
   - `👍` "More like this (the agent learns from it)" and `👎` "Less like this (the agent learns from it)". A chip the owner has used shows its count and a blue tint, so the vote is visibly on record.
   - `♥` like-back, only when the heart is offered (chapter 14). States: plain heart, enabled, tooltip "Like-back: heart the original post on <platform> (the agent's only write)"; pending, disabled, tooltip "Sending the heart to <platform>"; sent, `❤️`, disabled, tooltip "Heart sent to <platform> (the agent's only write)"; failed, enabled again with the reason in the tooltip.
   - An external-link glyph (14 px) that opens the original post in a new tab, tooltip and label "Open the original post on <platform>".
   - `unhide`, in hidden mode only, tooltip "Release this post back to the feed".
   - The category keys joined with ` · ` in faint small text, taking the width that is left and clipping with an ellipsis, tooltip `LLM categories, score <n>`. An unscored post shows none.
   - The post id `#1234` in faint text, tooltip "Internal post id; quote it when giving feedback".
   - Last, the `why` chip (tooltip "The decisions behind this post: your rules, verdict, view"). It opens the decision trail (chapter 10) as a bulleted list on a line of its own under the whole row, without moving the layout of other cards.

## 15.7 The stack

A folded burst (chapter 10) renders as one unit:

- The lead card is a normal card with the orange badge (`5 posts this week`).
- A sheet sticks out from under the lead card: 12 px up under the card, inset 12 px on each side, so the burst reads as cards behind this one and the line cannot belong to anything else. Its tint is the orange of the badge over dark glass, with an orange border, a chevron (down when closed, up when open), and the text `<n> more posts` in the badge color at a heavier weight. Tooltip: "The author's other posts of this burst, folded behind this card". The lead card paints over the sheet's hidden top edge.
- Opening the sheet unfolds the other cards in place, newest first, at the full column width, each 12 px below the previous. The unfold grows only its own column. It must work without script.

## 15.8 The lightbox

A click on an image or a tile opens it uncropped over a dark blurred overlay that fills the viewport, above the top bar and the dropdowns. The overlay keeps a 4% margin on every side, and the item fits inside it, with a 10 px radius and a large shadow.

- For a post with several items the overlay is a gallery. It navigates by round previous and next buttons (52 px, near the edges), by the left and right keys, by a swipe of more than 40 px on touch, and by a click on the left or right half of an image. A counter `i / n` sits at the bottom. Navigation wraps. With one item the buttons and the counter are hidden, and a click on the image closes.
- Space moves to the next item for an image. For a video it pauses and resumes.
- A video plays on arrival with native controls. When the slide changes or the box closes it is paused and fully unloaded, so no download outlives its slide. Clicks on the player do not close the box. A swipe that starts on the player is a seek, not a page turn.
- The `+N` tile opens the gallery at the first item the tiles skip.
- A click on the backdrop, or Escape, closes it.

## 15.9 Actions in place

The page must not move under the reader.

- **A thumb** updates only its own chip: the count goes up by one and the chip takes the voted tint. No reload.
- **A heart** changes its chip at once to the pending state, then asks the host for the like state every 3 seconds for up to 90 seconds (5 seconds after a network error). `sent` fills the heart. `failed` makes it pressable again with the reason in the tooltip. After 90 seconds without an end state, the pending chip stays until the next reload. A rejected press (the host answered with a problem) reloads the page and restores the scroll position.
- **Unhide** changes the list, so it reloads the page and restores the scroll position.
- **Mark all read** and the collect actions reload the page.
- Without script, every action is a plain POST and a redirect back.

## 15.10 The debug page

The same glass look, cards stacked in one column, a small uppercase soft-accent heading per card, compact tables with uppercase muted headers. The page refreshes itself every 10 seconds. Its header is the toolbar's head block with a bug mark in place of the sunrise, the wordmark `debug`, a note `auto-refresh 10 s · render <n> ms`, and a link back to the feed on the right. Banners at the top: an instance file error (serving the last good files), a scheduler tick failure, and one per troubled close-friend timeline (`Timeline of <name> (<platform>) did not render on the last <n> visits. Page said: <note>`).

Cards, in order:

1. scheduler: state, last tick, zone, CLI command, children, slots;
2. collect gates and processing;
3. recovery and upstream warnings;
4. recent runs;
5. the log tail, newest first, in a dark scroll box with links to the full CLI and web logs;
6. run requests;
7. posts by platform, with the thumbs counts;
8. the like-back queue;
9. platform state;
10. the live config;
11. locks;
12. kv;
13. coverage and timelines: current sweep progress and unvisited/stale/incomplete targets; name linked to the profile, last visit, status, failures in a row, what the page said, a screenshot link. Keep this potentially long table last so other diagnostics remain easy to reach.

Status cells are colored pills: running amber, ok and done green, error and refused red, pending blue, checkpoint dark amber, capped and imported teal. Descriptive status labels use a neutral badge; only known status values select a semantic CSS class, so label words cannot apply unrelated page styles. Every link on the page takes the soft accent, never the browser's default blue.

The debug page links to `/debug/backgrounds`. That gallery uses the same glass styling, with lazy thumbnails from one combined pool. Each tile names the image and source and offers Pin and Remove; current and pinned states are visible. Page-level Unpin and Next image actions use ordinary forms. Show the blur setting and latest download result. Next changes the selected image on the next page load; a pin remains across restarts. Do not auto-refresh this gallery while the owner is choosing an image. Downloads never run as a side effect of opening it.

## 15.11 The page footer

A quiet centered line closes the page: `chronological · your rules · personal use · health`. The word `health` links to the health route.

## 15.12 Qualities that must hold

- **Honest controls.** Every button states what will happen. Running, requested, disabled and failed states tell the truth, with the reason in the tooltip.
- **Explains itself.** Every card can show why it is here and, in hidden mode, why it is hidden.
- **No jumps.** An expand, a thumb, a heart, an image load or a resize never moves the card the owner is reading.
- **Fast.** Target useful text/cards painted in well under a second on the documented local-network benchmark with hundreds of cards, and smooth scrolling. Measure browser paint and layout separately from server render time; lazy image completion is not the first-paint measure. Record desktop/phone conditions and regressions. The large-cap behavior remains correct even when its explicitly larger page budget costs more time.
- **Phone-ready.** One column, tighter padding, the one-row bar, touch navigation in the lightbox, the safe area respected at the top.
- **Readable without script.** Text, links to all media, dropdowns, full-text/stack expansion and form actions work. Masonry, the lightbox and in-place updates are JavaScript enhancements.
- **One accent, one glass, one warm sun.** Nothing else competes.

Recovery details must be readable without opening log files: show affected post/capture, failure cause, retry eligibility and any paused or blocked state at `/debug#recovery`. The Debug menu link shows a small dot for current failures or recent upstream warnings; these do not create a normal-feed banner. Coverage labels distinguish `partial capture with errors` from `rendered but no matching post payload`, and show delayed/exhausted retries. Exact repeated-content groups use an `N repeated posts` badge and preserve expandable original cards and their actions.
